How a download goes

To begin, paste a link, or on Android share one from any app. EqDownload asks yt-dlp what the link holds and shows the title and the thumbnail.

youtube.com/watch?v=jNQXAC9IVRw

Before you ask

Why is there no iPhone version?

iOS does not let an app run yt-dlp the way Windows and Android do, and building and signing an iPhone app needs Apple’s paid developer account. None is planned.

How do I use it from my iPhone?

You do not use it on the iPhone. Press Send this page to my PC at the top, open the page on your computer or Android phone, and download the file there. Files you save on that device can reach your iPhone through AirDrop, a cable or your usual cloud folder.

Why the warnings, and is the file genuine?

GitHub asks you to sign in because the repository is private. Windows shows an unknown-publisher warning because the exe is not code-signed: choose More info, then Run anyway. To check a file, compare its SHA-256 with these values from the release.

Does it work with YouTube?

On your own connection, yes. Some networks get a bot check from YouTube, and EqDownload then shows the reason yt-dlp reports. When a site changes, press Update engine in Settings.

What does it send about me?

Nothing. It has no ads, accounts, analytics or crash reports. It contacts the site you pasted and, only when you press Update engine, GitHub.

Is the Android app tested?

It builds, is signed, and passes its unit tests. It has not been run on a phone yet. Treat it as untested until you try it, and report what breaks on the GitHub page.
